All blog posts in Loopspell live in the `/_posts/` directory of your GitHub repository as Markdown files. Every file you add, edit, or delete in that folder is reflected on your live blog within seconds — no build commands, no deploys, no terminal required.

## The Post Filename Format

Every post filename must follow this exact pattern:

```
YYYY-MM-DD-post-title.md
```

The date prefix tells Jekyll when the post was published, and the slug after the date becomes part of the post's URL. Here are a few valid examples:

```
2024-01-15-hello-world.md
2024-03-22-my-thoughts-on-coffee.md
2024-12-01-year-in-review.md
```

<Warning>
  Posts with a future date in their filename will **not** appear on your live blog until that date arrives. GitHub Pages rebuilds your site daily, so the post will go live automatically — but if you want a post visible immediately, make sure the date is today or in the past.
</Warning>

## Required Front Matter

Every post must start with a front matter block — a short YAML header fenced by triple dashes. At minimum, include `layout` and `title`:

```markdown
---
layout: post
title: Your Post Title Here
---
```

- **`layout: post`** tells Jekyll to wrap your content in the post template, which adds the title, date, and comment section automatically.
- **`title`** sets the heading displayed at the top of the post and in the browser tab.

You can also include these optional fields to organise your content:

```markdown
---
layout: post
title: Your Post Title Here
date: 2024-06-10
categories: [writing, tutorials]
tags: [jekyll, markdown, blogging]
---
```

- **`date`** overrides the date in the filename if you need to specify a time as well (e.g. `2024-06-10 09:00:00`).
- **`categories`** groups your post into one or more broad topics.
- **`tags`** attaches more granular labels to the post.

Add your content in Markdown on the line immediately after the closing `---`.

## Creating a New Post

<Steps>
  <Step title="Navigate to /_posts/">
    Open your repository on GitHub.com and click into the `/_posts/` directory.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the new-file dialog">
    Click the **`+`** icon or the **`Add file`** button, then choose **`Create new file`** from the dropdown.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name the file">
    Type the filename in the name field at the top of the editor using the required format — for example, `2024-06-10-my-new-post.md`. GitHub will place the file in `/_posts/` automatically.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the front matter block">
    Paste the following at the very top of the file, replacing the title with your own:

    ```markdown
    ---
    layout: post
    title: My New Post
    ---
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write your content">
    Below the closing `---`, write your post in Markdown. See the [Markdown Guide](/writing/markdown-guide) for syntax reference.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Commit the file">
    Scroll down to the **Commit changes** panel, optionally add a commit message, and click **Commit changes**. Your post is live on your blog within seconds.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Editing an Existing Post

1. Navigate to `/_posts/` in your repository and click the post file you want to edit.
2. Click the **pencil icon** (✏️) in the top-right corner of the file view to open the browser editor.
3. Make your changes in the editor.
4. Scroll down to the **Commit changes** panel and click **Commit changes** to save. The updated post appears on your blog within seconds.

## Deleting a Post

1. Navigate to `/_posts/` and click the post file you want to remove.
2. Click the **trash icon** (🗑) in the top-right corner of the file view.
3. Confirm the deletion in the **Commit changes** dialog and click **Commit changes**.

The post disappears from your live blog immediately after GitHub Pages finishes rebuilding — typically within a few seconds.

## Three Ways to Edit

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="GitHub.com">
    The built-in browser editor at GitHub.com is the simplest option. Navigate to any file in your repository, click the pencil icon to edit, and commit your changes — no software required. This is the recommended approach for most Loopspell users.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Prose.io">
    [Prose.io](http://prose.io) is a third-party editor built specifically for Jekyll sites hosted on GitHub. It provides a cleaner writing interface, a toolbar for Markdown formatting, drag-and-drop image uploads, and a dedicated drafts view. Authenticate with your GitHub account at prose.io to get started.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Local Clone">
    For advanced users, clone your repository to your local machine with `git clone`, edit files in any text editor, and push changes back to GitHub. GitHub Pages rebuilds and publishes your site automatically after each push.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>
