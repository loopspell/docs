Loopspell, powered by Jekyll, gives you two built-in tools for controlling when your content goes public: **scheduled posts** let you set a future publish date directly in the filename, and **drafts** give you a private holding area for work-in-progress content that is never published until you are ready. Neither feature requires any configuration changes — they work out of the box.

## Scheduling a Post

Jekyll determines a post's publish date from the date prefix in its filename. Any post with a **future date** is silently excluded from the built site and will not appear on your blog until that date arrives.

To schedule a post, simply name the file with a future date:

```
2099-12-25-my-future-post.md
```

GitHub Pages rebuilds your site on a regular schedule, so you do not need to trigger anything manually — the post goes live automatically on the specified date.

<Note>
  GitHub Pages build jobs run on a schedule, not precisely at midnight. A scheduled post may appear a few hours after midnight on the publish date rather than at exactly 00:00. If you need the post live at a specific time, publish it manually by backdating the filename to today's date when you are ready.
</Note>

## Using the \_drafts Folder

Jekyll recognises a special `/_drafts/` directory at the root of your repository. Files placed in `/_drafts/` are **never included in the live site**, regardless of their content or modification date. Draft filenames do not require the date prefix — just use a descriptive slug:

```
/_drafts/my-work-in-progress.md
```

Draft files still need a front matter block with at minimum `layout` and `title`:

```markdown
---
layout: post
title: My Work in Progress
---

Content goes here...
```

### Creating the \_drafts folder on GitHub.com

GitHub.com does not let you create an empty folder directly, but you can create the folder and your first draft file in one step:

1. Navigate to the root of your repository.
2. Click **`Add file`** → **`Create new file`**.
3. In the filename field at the top of the editor, type `_drafts/my-draft.md`. GitHub automatically creates the `_drafts/` folder when it sees the `/` in the name.
4. Add your front matter and content, then click **Commit changes**.

Your draft is saved privately and will not appear on your live blog.

## Moving a Draft to Published

When your draft is ready to publish, rename it from the `_drafts/` folder to `_posts/` and add the date prefix:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the draft file">
    Navigate to `/_drafts/` in your repository and click the draft file you want to publish.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the editor">
    Click the **pencil icon** (✏️) to open the browser editor.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Update the filename">
    Click into the **filename field** at the top of the editor (it shows the current path, e.g. `_drafts/my-draft.md`) and change the path to a `_posts/` path with today's date prefix — for example:

    ```
    _posts/2024-01-15-my-draft.md
    ```

    Typing the new path moves the file to the correct folder automatically.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Commit the change">
    Scroll down and click **Commit changes**. The post is now live on your blog with the date you specified.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  For a richer draft-management experience, try [Prose.io](http://prose.io). Prose displays your `/_drafts/` files in a dedicated **Drafts** section separate from published posts, making it easy to see what is in progress, edit draft content, and promote a draft to published — all without touching filenames manually.
</Tip>
