Loopspell automatically generates a standards-compliant Atom feed at `/feed.xml` powered by the `jekyll-feed` gem. Readers can subscribe using any RSS reader — no extra configuration is needed to generate the feed.

## Your Feed URL

Your feed is available at the following URL as soon as your blog is live:

```
https://yourusername.github.io/feed.xml
```

If you have set up a custom domain, use that instead:

```
https://yourblog.com/feed.xml
```

Share this URL with readers so they can subscribe directly in their RSS reader of choice.

## Adding the RSS Footer Icon

To display an RSS icon in your blog's footer that links to your feed, add any value to the `rss` key under `footer-links` in `_config.yml`:

```yaml _config.yml
footer-links:
  rss: true
```

Save the file and wait for the rebuild to complete. The RSS icon appears in your footer and links readers directly to your feed URL.

## What the Feed Contains

The Atom feed automatically includes the following for each post:

- **Post title** and **publication date**
- **Post excerpt or full content**, depending on your theme settings
- **Permalink** back to the original post on your blog

RSS readers such as [Feedly](https://feedly.com), [NetNewsWire](https://netnewswire.com), and [Reeder](https://reederapp.com) can subscribe to your feed URL and sync new posts automatically as you publish them.

## Submitting to Feed Directories

Increase your blog's reach by promoting your feed in a few places:

- **Submit to Feedly**: Visit [feedly.com](https://feedly.com) and add your feed URL so readers can find and follow your blog through their Feedly account.
- **Autodiscovery**: Loopspell already includes the feed autodiscovery `<link>` tag in every page's `<head>`, so RSS readers that support autodiscovery detect your feed automatically when a reader visits your blog.
- **Add it to your About page**: Mention your feed URL on your blog's About page so visitors know they can subscribe and stay up to date.

<Note>
  Your feed is regenerated on every GitHub Pages build. New posts appear in subscribers' RSS readers within minutes of you publishing — as soon as the GitHub Pages build finishes and the updated `feed.xml` is live.
</Note>
