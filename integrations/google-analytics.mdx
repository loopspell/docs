Loopspell includes built-in support for Google Analytics. Once you add your tracking ID to `_config.yml`, every page on your blog automatically sends analytics data — no template editing required.

## Getting Your Tracking ID

<Steps>
  <Step title="Sign in to Google Analytics">
    Go to [analytics.google.com](https://analytics.google.com) and sign in with your Google account.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a new property">
    Create a new property for your blog URL. Follow the setup prompts and enter your blog's address when asked for a website URL.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find your ID">
    Locate your tracking ID once the property is created:
    - **GA4 format** (Measurement ID): `G-XXXXXXXXXX`
    - **Universal Analytics format** (Tracking ID): `UA-XXXXXXXX-X`

    Copy the ID — you'll paste it into `_config.yml` in the next step.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Enabling Tracking

Open your `_config.yml` file and add your tracking ID to the `google_analytics` key:

```yaml _config.yml
google_analytics: UA-000000-2
```

Replace `UA-000000-2` with your actual tracking ID. Save the file to trigger a GitHub Pages rebuild. Analytics begins tracking visitor activity immediately after the site finishes rebuilding.

## Verifying the Setup

Confirm that tracking is working correctly after the rebuild:

1. Open your blog in an **incognito or private browsing window** to generate a fresh session that Google Analytics can detect.
2. Browse one or two pages on your blog.
3. Wait about one minute, then open Google Analytics and navigate to **Reports → Realtime**.
4. Confirm that your visit appears in the active users count.

If no data appears after a few minutes, double-check that the tracking ID in `_config.yml` matches the one shown in your Google Analytics property settings.

## Privacy Considerations

<Note>
  Analytics tracking collects data about your readers' devices, locations, and browsing behaviour. Disclose this in a privacy policy or cookie notice on your blog, as required by GDPR, CCPA, and similar data-protection regulations. Many jurisdictions require you to obtain reader consent before activating tracking.
</Note>
